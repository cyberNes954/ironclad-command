# Ironclad Core Operations Reference

## ACCESSORIAL RATE SCHEDULE
Detention dry: $50/hr after first 2 hours, max $250/day
Detention frozen: $75/hr after first 2 hours, max $250/day
Detention produce and fresh meat: $75/hr after first 2 hours, max $250/day
Detention flatbed: $75/hr after first 2 hours, max $250/day
Layover: $250 per day
TONU (truck ordered not used): $150
Redelivery: negotiated based on miles
Unloading/lumper: exact amount, ALL receipts required or no pay

### Three rules that decide whether a detention claim gets paid
1. Notify the broker by call or email THE MOMENT detention starts. Silent waiting = denied claim.
2. Driver must arrive within the FCFS window. Early or late forfeits the claim.
3. Everything is negotiable AT BOOKING TIME, not after delivery.
Note: the $250/day cap means a 6-8 hour unload pays at most $250. Price the linehaul up or decline.

## COST PER MILE (CPM)
Formula: Total CPM = Fixed CPM + Variable CPM
Fixed CPM = monthly fixed costs / realistic monthly miles (include deadhead)
Variable CPM = fuel + maintenance + tires + DEF + tolls + IFTA
Fuel CPM = current diesel price / REAL mpg (not advertised mpg)
Target rate = CPM + 15 to 25 percent profit margin

### Break-even benchmarks by equipment
Dry van: $1.20-$1.40/mi -> target $1.40-$1.75
Reefer: $1.45-$1.65/mi -> target $1.65-$2.05
Flatbed: $1.40-$1.60/mi -> target $1.60-$2.00
Box truck: $0.85-$1.05/mi -> target $1.00-$1.30
Hotshot: $1.45-$1.70/mi -> target $1.65-$2.15

### Worked example
Fixed $4,000/month / 10,000 miles = $0.40
Diesel $4.50 / 7.5 mpg = $0.60
Maintenance $0.18 + tires $0.06 + DEF/tolls/IFTA $0.10 = $0.34
Total CPM = $1.34 break-even

### Variable cost industry averages
Maintenance $0.15-$0.25/mi | Tires $0.05-$0.08/mi | DEF $0.03-$0.05/mi
Tolls $0.03-$0.10/mi (Northeast highest) | IFTA $0.02-$0.05/mi

### CRITICAL: minimum rate must be GROSS of the dispatch fee
If carrier needs $1.86 net and fee is 8 percent, minimum gross = 1.86 / 0.92 = $2.02/mi.
Setting the floor at the carrier's net number means the fee eats their profit.

### Deadhead math
500-mile load at $2.00 with 100 deadhead miles = $1,000 / 600 total miles = $1.67 effective.
Always evaluate on ALL miles, never loaded miles only.

### Owner-operator coaching point
Include a market-rate driver wage even when the owner drives. Otherwise their
"profit" is just unpaid wages for their own labor.

## GOLDEN OPERATING RULES
No Rate Confirmation = No Dispatch. No exceptions.
If it is not written on the Rate Confirmation, it does not exist.
No forced dispatch, ever. Carrier approves every load.
POD to broker within 2 hours of delivery.
Notify broker of any delay within 15 minutes with new ETA and action plan.
Check in with driver within 30 minutes of pickup.
Never state your rate first. Let the broker anchor, then counter with CPM-backed numbers.

## EQUIPMENT SPECS
### 53 ft Dry Van High Cube
Inside 52.4 ft L x 110 in H x 98.7 in W at scuff
Door opening 110 in H x 98.3 in W | Dock height 50 in
Max cargo 43,500 lbs | 26 pallets | 3,500 cu ft
48 ft high cube: 45,000 lbs, 24 pallets, 3,480 cu ft
45 ft van: 45,000 lbs, 21 pallets, 3,000 cu ft

### Open deck (legal freight limits)
Flatbed 48 ft: 45,000-48,000 lbs, 48 ft length, 8.5 ft (102 in) height
Step deck: 40,000-42,500 lbs, 37 ft lower + 11 ft upper, 10 ft (120 in) height on lower deck
Double drop / lowboy: 38,000-40,000 lbs, 28 ft well, 11.5 ft (138 in) height
WIDTH LIMIT IS 102 IN (8.5 FT) ON ALL THREE. Anything wider is oversize, needs permits.
Legal overall height 13 ft 6 in. Lower deck = taller freight allowed, but less payload.
Decision tree: under 8.5 ft tall = flatbed | 8.5-10 ft = step deck | 10-11.5 ft = double drop | taller = OD permit

### Box truck
16 / 20 / 26 ft | 8-12 pallets typical | confirm dock-high vs liftgate every time
Confirm pallet jack, straps, load bars, e-track, driver assist capability

### Reefer
Insulated trailer with active cooling. Can also HEAT (protect from freeze).
Diesel-powered unit burns its own fuel - that is the separate reefer fuel cost line.
Fresh produce and dairy 34-38 F | Fresh meat and poultry 28-32 F
Frozen 0 to -10 F | Ice cream -20 F or colder | Pharma 36-46 F strict monitoring
Never mix temperatures. If temps don't match, don't stack.
Temperature must be written on the RC, typically plus/minus 2 F variance.
No odor mixing - onions, seafood, cilantro contaminate everything.
Never block the air chute or return air bulkhead. Palletized only, airflow space at doors.
Late delivery = rejected freight. Reefer rates run 20-40 percent above dry van.

## PARTIAL LOADS
Partial = 30-70 percent of capacity, roughly 6-20 pallets depending on equipment.
Box truck partials typically 1-10 pallets, dock-to-dock, sometimes liftgate.
A truck grossing $2,000 on a full load can gross $3,000-$4,000 combining compatible partials.
One preventable mistake costs $1,000-$5,000.
Partials pay for TIME and ACCESSORIALS, not mileage. Short runs need a minimum dollar figure.
Flatbed: first on = last off. One load needs a tarp = everything gets tarped.
Auto-decline or reprice: unpaid driver assist, residential + liftgate + inside combos,
tight 8am windows, "weight unknown" or "pallet count TBD".

## CARRIER VETTING - 5 POINT SAFER CHECK
1. Entity Type must read CARRIER. Broker-affiliated = double brokering risk, skip.
2. Authority Status must be ACTIVE.
3. MC Date - note authority age. Many brokers reject MCs under 60-90 days.
   Workaround: 3 good references from successful loads in the last 90 days.
4. Safety Rating: Satisfactory or Unrated = OK. Conditional = flag/skip.
   Unsatisfactory = HARD NO, carrier is generally prohibited from operating (49 CFR 385.13).
   Over 80 percent of carriers are UNRATED - that just means no audit yet, NOT a red flag.
5. Power Units 1-5 = owner-operator target. 20+ = has in-house dispatch, disqualify.

Tools: SAFER snapshot at safer.fmcsa.dot.gov/CompanySnapshot.aspx
New carrier leads: li-public.fmcsa.dot.gov/LIVIEW/PKG_REGISTER.prc_reg_list
MC certificate is static paper. ALWAYS verify live status on SAFER, never trust the PDF alone.
Certificate stays valid only while insurance (49 CFR 387) and process agents/BOC-3 (49 CFR 366) are maintained.
Standard property authority EXCLUDES household goods.

## FACTORING AND BONDS
Factoring = finance company buys your invoices at a discount.
Ask: application fee (up to $500), must I factor 100 percent, finance charge per invoice (up to 5 percent),
contract length (1-5 years), exit clause, recourse (carrier pays) vs non-recourse (factor pays).
Brokers pay lumpers and fuel advances through EFS-type setups.
Broker surety bond is $75,000 under MAP-21. Annual cost $750-$9,000.
Bond price driven by credit, industry experience, bond type.
